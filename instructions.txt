5. Proposed Research Direction
Working title: Lesion-Scale-Aware Boundary Preservation under Speckle Corruption

The motivation is that small ultrasound lesions often have weak contrast, irregular boundaries, and noise-sensitive edges. A model may achieve reasonable overall Dice while missing small lesions or producing inaccurate boundaries. We will investigate whether a lightweight training strategy can improve these cases without replacing the UltraLight VM-UNet architecture.

The proposed extension will investigate three components:

Lesion-scale-aware boundary supervision: Use the ground-truth mask during training to identify lesion boundaries and lesion-size categories. Investigate whether boundary supervision should be adjusted according to lesion size.

Corruption-aware consistency training: Apply controlled ultrasound-like corruption during training and investigate whether the model can preserve useful segmentation predictions under speckle-like noise and reduced contrast.

Lightweight deployment: Keep the original UltraLight VM-UNet inference architecture unchanged initially. The additional training objectives should not require an extra branch during inference.

The central research question is:

Can lesion-scale-aware boundary supervision combined with corruption-aware consistency training improve small-lesion segmentation and boundary robustness across breast, thyroid, and ovarian ultrasound datasets without increasing inference complexity?

This is a proposed research hypothesis, not an established contribution. We must examine existing boundary-aware, corruption-robust, and lesion-scale-aware segmentation methods to determine whether this combination provides a sufficiently distinct contribution.
